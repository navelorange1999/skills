# Practical Books

**What makes them distinct:** Practical books tell you what to do, or how to
do it. They aim at action, not just understanding. Ethics, politics,
self-help, economics, management, engineering how-tos, diet guides.

## Rule modifications

- **Rule 2 (unity):** Find not just the theme but the *end* the author is
  advocating and the *means* they recommend.
- **Rule 4 (problems):** Practical books address *practical problems* —
  problems about action in some sphere. Identify the sphere, the problem,
  and the author's proposed course of action.
- **Rule 5 (terms):** Key terms include the author's *value terms* — what
  they call good, desirable, successful. These are often the most
  smuggled-in, because practical authors rarely defend their ends; they
  assume them.
- **Rule 8 (solved vs. unsolved):** Ask whether the means actually achieve
  the ends. Practical books often under-examine this.

## Critical-stage pitfalls

- **Smuggled values.** Every practical book rests on a conception of the
  good. If the author doesn't defend it, you must name it — and decide
  whether you share it.
- **"Follow my advice" is not a syllogism.** A practical book hasn't
  persuaded you until (a) you understand the end, (b) you agree the end is
  worth pursuing, and (c) you judge the means effective. Disagreement can
  lodge at any of the three.
