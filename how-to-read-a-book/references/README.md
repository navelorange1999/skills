# Reading by Book Type — Genre-Specific Rules

Adler's eleven analytical rules apply to every book, but their *application*
shifts by genre. A philosophy book demands different moves than a novel, and
a scientific paper demands different moves than a history. This directory
spells out the genre-specific rules that the main `SKILL.md` points to.

Use these rules **on top of** the eleven analytical rules, not instead of
them. When a user brings you a book, first classify it (Rule 1), then open
the relevant file below and fold its rules into your protocol.

## Cross-Genre Notes

- **Mixed-genre works exist.** Many serious books are part philosophy,
  part history, part social science (Foucault, Marx, Tocqueville). Apply
  the rules from each relevant genre; don't force a single classification.

- **When in doubt, use the practical-book test.** Ask: is this book asking
  me to *do* something, or to *know* something? Practical books live in
  the first category; most everything else in the second.

- **Syntopical reading crosses genres.** When you're doing syntopical
  reading on, say, "what is freedom," you will read philosophers,
  historians, economists, and novelists. Each gets its own genre-specific
  moves; all feed into Step 2 (bring the authors to terms) and Step 4
  (define the issues).
