# Philosophy

**What makes it distinct:** Philosophy asks the fundamental questions —
questions that can't be settled by running an experiment or looking up a
fact. The answers are arguments, and the arguments turn on the meaning of
terms. Philosophical reading is slow, careful, and demanding.

## Rule modifications

- **Rule 1 (classify):** What kind of philosophical work? Dialogue,
  treatise, essay, aphorism, fragments? Each has different conventions.
  A Platonic dialogue is not read the way a Kantian treatise is.
- **Rule 4 (problems):** Identify the **controlling question** the
  philosopher is trying to answer. Philosophical works almost always have
  one. *What is justice?* (Plato). *How is synthetic a priori knowledge
  possible?* (Kant). *What is the meaning of Being?* (Heidegger). Miss
  the question and you'll misread every answer.
- **Rule 5 (terms):** Philosophical terms are the hardest to come to
  terms on. Philosophers often use ordinary words in technical ways
  (*substance*, *idea*, *being*, *form*). Watch for the moment a
  familiar word starts doing unfamiliar work.
- **Rule 7 (arguments):** Philosophical arguments are long and
  interlocking. Later propositions rest on earlier ones. Read carefully
  enough that you can reconstruct the argument, not just recognize it.

## Tactics

- **Read the philosopher on their own terms first.** Defer criticism
  strictly until you have reconstructed the position faithfully.
- **Think for yourself.** Philosophy is not an empirical field where you
  defer to experts. After you have understood, *you* must judge. Use
  the four grounds. "I disagree" is not enough; "the argument has a gap
  at step N because…" is.
- **Take the dialogue form seriously.** In Plato, Socrates's interlocutors
  often say things Plato means to undermine. Don't take every utterance
  as the author's view.

## Critical-stage pitfalls

- **Anachronism.** Don't project later distinctions onto earlier works.
  A pre-Kantian philosopher isn't making a Kantian mistake; they're
  working in a different frame.
- **Scientism.** Philosophy is not bad science. Judging Aristotle's
  metaphysics by the standards of modern physics is a category error.
- **Lazy relativism.** "It's all just opinion" is not a critical
  judgment; it is a refusal to make one. Philosophy demands judgment.
