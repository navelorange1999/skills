# Social Science

**What makes it distinct:** Social science sits uneasily between the
empirical sciences and the humanities. Economics uses math like physics;
anthropology uses narrative like history; sociology does both. This
mixed-genre status is the central difficulty.

## Rule modifications

- **Rule 1 (classify):** Which social science and which *tradition within
  it*? A quantitative economics paper and a qualitative anthropology
  monograph are in the same nominal field but are entirely different
  genres.
- **Rule 5 (terms):** Social-scientific terms are often *politically
  loaded* ("class," "race," "development," "rationality"). Different
  schools use the same term for different concepts. Be a translator, not
  a partisan.
- **Rule 7 (arguments):** Check whether the argument is empirical
  (evidence-based) or theoretical (frame-based). Conflating the two is a
  common failure.

## Critical-stage pitfalls

- **Pseudo-consensus.** Social sciences often *look* settled from inside a
  school and *look* contested from outside it. Don't mistake in-group
  agreement for disciplinary consensus.
- **Jargon drift.** Key terms accumulate meanings across decades of
  citation. "Habitus" in 2025 is not "habitus" in Bourdieu's original
  usage. Check the author's definition, not the received one.
- **The generalization question.** Social science often generalizes from
  one context (a Western country, a particular decade) to humans at
  large. Watch for this. Often the four grounds lodge at *incomplete
  analysis*.
- **Values masquerading as findings.** Social science is not value-free,
  and the pretense that it is usually conceals the values. Name them.
