# Science and Mathematics

**What makes them distinct:** Scientific and mathematical works make
claims about the world (science) or about formal structures (mathematics)
and defend them with evidence or proof. The argument is the substance.

## Rule modifications

- **Rule 4 (problems):** In science, identify the hypothesis or the
  phenomenon being explained. In mathematics, identify the theorem being
  proved or the problem being solved.
- **Rule 5 (terms):** Scientific and mathematical terms are usually
  *defined operationally or formally*. Find the definitions — don't
  improvise them from ordinary usage.
- **Rule 6 (propositions):** These are the hypotheses, axioms, theorems,
  findings. Know which is which. A hypothesis is not a finding; an axiom
  is not a theorem.
- **Rule 7 (arguments):** The argument is the experiment (in science) or
  the proof (in mathematics). *Master the problem before the argument.*
  Don't get lost in symbols before you know what question the symbols are
  in service of.

## Tactics

- **In mathematics, do the problems.** A proof you read passively is not
  a proof you understand. Work examples with pencil and paper.
- **In experimental science, follow the experimental design.** Who was
  measured? What was controlled? What was the effect size? What's the
  failure mode the author didn't test for?
- **Don't worship math.** Heavy formalism is sometimes substance, sometimes
  ritual. If you can restate the result in plain language and it's trivial
  or vague, the math may be cover for thin content.

## Critical-stage pitfalls

- **In science: selection, confounding, and generalization.** The four
  grounds for disagreement here almost always lodge at *incomplete
  analysis* — what would falsify the claim? was the test adequate? does
  the result generalize beyond the sample?
- **In mathematics: proof errors, hidden assumptions.** If a proof skips
  steps, your job is to fill them in. If you can't, either the proof has
  a gap or you haven't yet understood it — distinguishing these is part
  of reading mathematics.
